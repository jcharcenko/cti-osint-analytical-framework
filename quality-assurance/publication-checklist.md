
# Intelligence Publication Quality Assurance Checklist

**Framework Version:** 1.0  
**Document Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Reusable Checklist

## Purpose

This checklist provides a structured final review of CTI and OSINT intelligence products before publication.

It is intended to identify unsupported claims, source evaluation weaknesses, inaccurate technical mappings, analytical bias and inconsistencies between evidence and conclusions.

A completed checklist should be retained with each investigation.

## 1. Intelligence Requirements and Scope

- [ ] The primary intelligence question is clearly defined.
- [ ] The assessment addresses the approved Priority Intelligence Requirements.
- [ ] Geographic, temporal and subject boundaries are stated.
- [ ] Material scope changes are documented.
- [ ] Conclusions do not extend beyond the available evidence.

## 2. Source Verification

- [ ] Every material factual claim has an identifiable supporting source.
- [ ] Original sources have been inspected wherever accessible.
- [ ] Inaccessible sources and verification limitations are disclosed.
- [ ] Publication dates and source details have been checked.
- [ ] Quotations accurately reproduce the source material.
- [ ] Translations preserve the original meaning.
- [ ] Links and references point to the intended evidence.
- [ ] Source reliability grades have recorded justifications.

## 3. Claim Evaluation and Corroboration

- [ ] Material claims are recorded in the claim register.
- [ ] Information credibility is evaluated separately from source reliability.
- [ ] Claims have supporting evidence references.
- [ ] Corroboration has been checked for source dependencies.
- [ ] Repeated reporting has not been misrepresented as independent confirmation.
- [ ] Contradictory evidence has been recorded and evaluated.
- [ ] Unverified claims are appropriately qualified.
- [ ] Important evidence gaps are disclosed.

## 4. Attribution

- [ ] Reported attribution is distinguished from independent analytical judgement.
- [ ] Attribution claims are linked to their originating sources.
- [ ] Public supporting evidence and limitations are identified.
- [ ] Official statements are not automatically treated as proof of responsibility.
- [ ] Claims of responsibility are not automatically treated as verified.
- [ ] Actor aliases and campaign relationships have been checked.
- [ ] Attribution confidence is consistent with the supporting evidence.

## 5. Technical Intelligence

- [ ] Technical findings are supported by incident-specific evidence.
- [ ] MITRE ATT&CK technique IDs and names have been verified.
- [ ] ATT&CK mappings accurately reflect documented behaviour.
- [ ] Explicitly reported, analyst-mapped and inferred techniques are distinguished.
- [ ] Inferred techniques are excluded from observed-TTP statistics.
- [ ] Malware, tool and vulnerability names are verified.
- [ ] Technical observations are not confused with actor attribution.
- [ ] Defensive implications follow logically from documented behaviour.

## 6. Dataset and Statistical Integrity

- [ ] Incident records meet the approved inclusion criteria.
- [ ] Duplicate incidents have been identified and handled.
- [ ] Incident, discovery and disclosure dates are not conflated.
- [ ] Unknown values have not been replaced with invented information.
- [ ] Charts and tables match the underlying datasets.
- [ ] Statistics are calculated using clearly defined denominators.
- [ ] Reporting bias and dataset limitations are disclosed.
- [ ] Public reporting frequency is not presented as actual attack frequency.

## 7. Analytical Judgements

- [ ] Key judgements are supported by identifiable evidence.
- [ ] Analytical inference is distinguished from established fact.
- [ ] Material alternative explanations have been considered.
- [ ] Conflicting perspectives are assessed against consistent criteria.
- [ ] High, Moderate or Low confidence ratings have clear rationales.
- [ ] Confidence is not confused with probability or likelihood.
- [ ] Significant uncertainties are explained.
- [ ] Forward-looking assessments are appropriately qualified.

## 8. AI-Assisted Research Controls

- [ ] No material claim relies solely on AI-generated information.
- [ ] AI-suggested sources and references have been independently checked.
- [ ] Dates, quotations, names and technical identifiers have been verified.
- [ ] AI-generated summaries preserve the meaning of original sources.
- [ ] Unsupported AI-generated assertions have been removed.
- [ ] Research gaps have not been filled with fabricated information.
- [ ] Final conclusions have undergone analyst review.

## 9. Publication and Professional Standards

- [ ] The executive assessment accurately reflects the detailed analysis.
- [ ] The report uses clear, professional and neutral language.
- [ ] Citations and references are consistent.
- [ ] The framework version is identified.
- [ ] The methodology and limitations are described.
- [ ] Sensitive or inappropriate information has been excluded.
- [ ] Spelling, grammar, formatting and links have been reviewed.
- [ ] The final report is suitable for its intended audience.

## 10. Final Evidence Audit

For each key judgement:

1. Identify the supporting claim IDs.
2. Trace the claims to their source IDs.
3. Reinspect the most important supporting evidence.
4. Review contradictory evidence and alternative explanations.
5. Confirm the confidence rationale.
6. Verify that the published wording does not overstate the evidence.
